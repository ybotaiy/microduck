## What is advertised, and what is actually established

The advertised robot includes one front camera, an 8×8 ToF LiDAR, and two IMUs (body and head). These are presented as robot specifications, not accessory-pack upgrades. However, the manufacturer explicitly marks camera resolution/FOV and LiDAR range as provisional. Mechanical and electronic designs are not published as open-source hardware. A verified production BOM and the components fitted to an individual delivered unit therefore remain unknown. [Official product](https://pollen-robotics.com/microduck/) · [Official press kit](https://pollen-robotics.com/microduck/press-kit/)

The source below describes the alpha implementation. A driver supporting a chip, or a daemon accepting a subscription, does not prove that a particular unit has a functioning sensor. ToF and head-IMU subscription responses separately identify the detected chip and report unavailability. Support for missing sensors is not evidence that the advertised production sensors are optional purchases.

## Optics, geometry and timing details that matter

The camera source records a September 8 hardware calibration correcting an earlier crop assumption: pinned 1080p uses the full approximately 62° **horizontal** field, scaled and cropped to 16:9. Family intrinsics are distinguishable from per-unit calibration, nominal estimates and simulation metadata. Pixels arrive with a quarter-turn mounting correction described separately; rotating pixels requires rotating intrinsics too. Older remote-video design notes have a conflicting crop explanation; prefer the dated camera correction for this alpha setup, while retaining the manufacturer's production caveat. [Camera implementation](https://github.com/pollen-robotics/microduck/blob/ad38eee1a271519b1af8a271c9ccfba8acada9c6/mediad/src/camera.rs)

The existing project camera is an idealized simulation configuration: 480×288, **70° vertical** FOV, fixed neutral-pose adjustment giving a 25° downward view. Those numbers are not measured hardware calibration and must not be carried into a real visibility envelope. Upstream's original MJCF camera defaults to 45° vertical. See [project camera code](sim/camera_view.py).

Both ST chip product pages specify up to 4 m range and 65° diagonal FOV; this is component capability, not a guaranteed robot working distance. The integration uses a 45°×45° beam model. The exact fitted generation, cover-glass effects, illumination, materials and zone validity must be checked on the unit. Do not convert the chip's maximum headline rate into the daemon's configured 8×8 rate. [VL53L5CX](https://www.st.com/en/imaging-and-photonics-solutions/vl53l5cx) · [VL53L8CX](https://www.st.com/en/imaging-and-photonics-solutions/vl53l8cx.html) · [Beam geometry](https://github.com/pollen-robotics/microduck/blob/ad38eee1a271519b1af8a271c9ccfba8acada9c6/kinematics/src/tof.rs)

The existing ToF reprojector distinguishes floor and obstacle hits using head pose, trunk gravity and measured/assumed height. It discards returns below about 10 cm as untrustworthy for this integration. Its simplified `Empty` class merges unavailable/invalid returns; a future edge detector must preserve the raw status distinction and cannot interpret that class as safe ground. Floor filtering is a geometric helper, not a demonstrated motion veto. [Reprojection source](https://github.com/pollen-robotics/microduck/blob/ad38eee1a271519b1af8a271c9ccfba8acada9c6/kinematics/src/tof.rs)

15 Hz depth, 50 Hz control and 100 Hz head-IMU defaults imply sampling periods of about 67, 20 and 10 ms; these are **not end-to-end latency measurements**. The video design reports approximately 29.3 fps on hardware at the tested 720p30 output, not a measured camera-to-stop delay. State, depth and head IMU share monotonic read timestamps; read time still differs from optical exposure/integration time. Measure capture age, transport, processing, command application and physical stopping together. [Video measurements](https://github.com/pollen-robotics/microduck/blob/ad38eee1a271519b1af8a271c9ccfba8acada9c6/docs/design/remote-webrtc.md) · [Protocol](https://github.com/pollen-robotics/microduck/blob/ad38eee1a271519b1af8a271c9ccfba8acada9c6/duck-ipc-proto/src/lib.rs)

## What can reasonably be attempted

**Obstacle warning: feasible to investigate with existing sensors.** This is an engineering inference from the available range, image and posture signals, not a verified safety capability. Start with objects that occupy multiple ToF zones inside the observed field. A conservative controller needs stale/invalid-data handling and a measured stopping envelope, not simply a range threshold.

**Drop-off warning: conditionally observable, not established as reliable.** A downward-looking range beam can test whether ground appears near its expected distance. Missing, farther or invalid returns should trigger uncertainty. Looking at a target can remove ground coverage; one head-mounted sensor cannot simultaneously guarantee coverage in every direction. IMUs and motor current generally react to motion/load changes and cannot replace advance support sensing. Flat-ground odometry must not be used as independent evidence that ground exists.

Calibration/software work may suffice for a constrained, slow, forward-only demonstration if existing beams observe the entire intended footfall/stopping region. Additional fixed downward range sensors become a reasonable design option if head motion or geometry leaves that region unobserved. Dense depth/stereo could improve scene detail, but added hardware needs mechanical, power and interface validation. No purchase decision is justified before measuring the actual coverage gap.

## Minimal next validation proposal (not executed or authorized by this document)

1. Identify the delivered revision, detected camera mode/intrinsics source, ToF generation and both IMUs. Save brief timestamped samples and unavailable/stale cases. No user input is needed to finish this documentary research; revision and sensor detection are the remaining unit-specific facts.
2. With the robot supported and stationary, check camera/ToF alignment against measured targets and a flat floor at several head angles. Include dark, reflective and narrow objects and a protected mock edge. Measure the nearest visible ground and the status of invalid returns; do not walk toward an unprotected drop.
3. Map valid ground coverage to the next footfall and swept body region. Require that coverage to exceed measured stopping travel plus motion during total sensing/command delay and a calibration margin. If coverage is insufficient, change allowed head/motion combinations or evaluate fixed downward sensing before further autonomy.
4. Only after the sensing check, propose a bounded motion-veto experiment with explicit pass/fail criteria: obstacle stop, loss-of-ground/unknown stop, stale-stream stop, manual stop and false-stop counts. Use realistic sensor observations for decisions and simulator truth only for evaluation.

The current ball-search controller uses simulator body pose for travel limits. Real contact odometry exists, but is an estimate with different failure modes; replacing simulated pose with it is unvalidated work.
