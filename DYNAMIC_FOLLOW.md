# Dynamic target following, without vision

The `follow` mode extends the stationary-ball experiment with a repeatable
moving target and a restartable stop. Robot motion is generated by the same
official ONNX policies and CPU MuJoCo XML position actuators. The target is a
scripted mocap body: its position is imposed, not the outcome of free rolling
or robot-ball contact dynamics. This is labeled on every video frame.

## Reproduce

Use the dependencies and pinned models in `SIMULATION.md`, then run from the
repository root:

```sh
microduck-lab/.venv/bin/python sim/experiment.py --mode follow --ball 0.6 0.05 --seconds 42 --video --out runs/dynamic-follow
python3 sim/test_follow.py
```

`--ball` is the initial ball center in world x/y coordinates. The relative
trajectory is in `sim/follow.py`; the exact keyframes and controller parameters
are saved in every summary. Remove `--video` for numerical-only runs.

| Time (s) | Scripted ball position (m) | Action |
|---|---|---|
| 0–8 | (0.60, 0.05) | Hold |
| 8–16 | Linear motion to (1.00, 0.23) | Move |
| 16–23 | (1.00, 0.23) | Hold |
| 23–31 | Linear motion to (1.37, -0.07) | Move in a different direction |
| 31–42 | (1.37, -0.07) | Hold |

The segment speeds are approximately 0.055 and 0.060 m/s (derived from
Euclidean segment length divided by eight seconds). Positions are continuous;
velocity changes immediately at the keyframes. The target is deliberately slow
enough for the existing walking policy in this tested setting.

## Control

At 50 Hz, read the target and robot ground-truth positions, compute a wrapped
bearing error, and request the same bounded walking turn as in the static mode.
At trunk-to-ball-center distance <= 0.26 m, request standing. Resume walking at
distance >= 0.38 m, after at least 0.8 s since the last state change. Proximity
stops are immediate; the dwell rule never postpones stopping.

The gap between stop and restart thresholds is hysteresis. Noise inside the
gap cannot toggle the state by itself. This simple follower alternates walking
and waiting while the ball moves; it does not continuously match target speed.
The static `ball` mode keeps its original permanent stop latch.

## Recorded result — September 9, 2026

`sim/results/dynamic-follow.json` stores the measured result and source/model
hashes. Full per-tick records include robot pose, speed, command, target position
and scripted velocity, distance, and state in `trajectory.jsonl` under the run
output directory. Events are also recorded in the summary.

- One 42-second deterministic scenario, run numerically and with video: identical
  final pose/distance and state-transition times.
- Five stops and four resumes. Both target departures triggered renewed pursuit.
- Stop/resume events at 4.96, 10.80, 12.90, 15.10, 16.70, 25.70, 27.58, 29.86,
  and 31.52 s. Minimum transition interval: 1.60 s; no rapid threshold chatter
  in this trace.
- Each hold interval ended with the robot stopped for its final second, with
  mean speed below 0.001 m/s.
- Distance after the first stop stayed at or below 0.3864 m. Minimum distance
  over the run: 0.2210 m; final distance: 0.2547 m. These are trunk-to-ball-center
  distances, not physical clearance measurements.
- No detected robot-ball contact or fall. Maximum trunk tilt: 6.70 degrees.
- Ground truth was always available. The `tracking_lost` diagnostic means more
  than two seconds at distance over 1 m after the initial approach; it never
  fired. It is not a visual object-loss test.

Success checks cover both departures, settling during each hold, final stop,
distance/speed, sustained separation, and fall/contact—not only the endpoint.
Four unit tests cover hysteresis under distance jitter, restart dwell versus
immediate stop, heading wraparound, and target continuity/holds. The static
left-front regression preserved final position, distance, speed, tilt, and
success exactly; existing static videos and results are retained.

## Limits

This is one slow path and initial pose, not a robustness estimate. Fast targets,
sharp reversals toward the robot, obstacles, noisy or missing localization, and
vision are untested. A faster target may escape and a ball moving toward the
robot may collide; the controller does not plan a retreat. Its stop/walk policy
switching produces visible bursts rather than smooth speed matching.

No camera, training, BAM comparison, or hardware deployment was added. The
ground-truth controller is a future comparison baseline for perception errors;
a camera bridge would be a separate next experiment.
